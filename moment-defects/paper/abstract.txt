Shevtsova's sharp third-moment inequality supplies an exact defect that identifies the standardized two-point family and controls transport and characteristic functions. We develop the corresponding Fourier reduction and give the qualitative eventual upper-bound argument. A penalized extremal problem yields eventual two-point optimality and a uniform linear deficit, with sharp cubic Wasserstein stability. A fifth-order lattice saddle gives the full oscillatory \(n^{-2}\) correction for the optimal constants. A separate effective argument proves \(C_n<C_{\mathrm E}\) for every \(n\ge31\), by finite-index certificates and a first-failure argument. A completed finite continuation at indices \(20,\ldots,24\) lowers the cutoff to 20 under the explicitly retained analytic and historical certificate premises stated in the paper. Exact polynomial certificates give bounds for two and three summands. For four summands, a common-measure Fourier comparison and rational certificates give the unrestricted bound \(C_4\le0.4097<C_{\mathrm E}\); a sharper estimate \(C_{4,\le3}<0.409\) holds on at most three points. An elementary historical bound \(C\le0.45\) explains the passage from a symmetric reference to the matching two-point law; its finite arithmetic is recorded in an appendix.
